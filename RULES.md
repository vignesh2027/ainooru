## Theme

"Build something worth launching." Open ended. Overview wording: "Find a real problem. Build a working solution. Show us what you learned. A focused prototype that does one thing well can be a great submission." Any stack is welcome, including low-code.

## Judging criteria (verbatim from the overview page)

Every criterion is scored 1 to 5 with equal weight. The highest total wins. Ties go first to Usefulness & Impact, then to Implementation & Functionality, then to a comparative panel review.

1. **Innovation & Originality.** "How original is the idea or approach? Does the project offer a fresh solution, thoughtful improvement, or creative use of technology? Judges score each criterion equally on a 1–5 scale."
2. **Usefulness & Impact.** "How clearly does the project address a real problem or opportunity? Consider its value to the intended users, evidence of user needs, and potential positive impact. Score 1–5; equal weight with the other criteria."
3. **Implementation & Functionality.** "How well does the working prototype deliver its core promise? Consider functionality, thoughtful technical choices, reliability, and what the team built during the event. Complexity alone does not earn a higher score. Score 1–5; equal weight."
4. **Design & Presentation.** "Is the project understandable and easy to use? Consider accessibility, the clarity of the user experience, and how clearly the submission explains the problem, solution, demo, and limitations. Score 1–5; equal weight."

The rules page confirms: "Eligible projects are assessed equally on Innovation & Originality, Usefulness & Impact, Implementation & Functionality, and Design & Presentation. Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5. Highest total scores determine the overall ranking. Ties are resolved first by Usefulness & Impact, then Implementation & Functionality, then a final comparative review by the judging panel."

## Submission requirements (verbatim lists from the overview)

What to build: "Build a working technology prototype that addresses a problem or opportunity you care about. Any technology stack is welcome, including low-code and no-code tools. Clearly identify the work completed during October 16–November 20, 2026."

What to submit:

* "A completed Devpost project page with a name, short pitch, and team members."
* "A description of the problem, intended users, solution, technologies, challenges, and what you learned."
* "A working demo link, downloadable build, or recorded walkthrough showing the core functionality. Include setup steps. Never include private credentials or personal data."
* "Screenshots of the main experience. A short demo video of about 2–3 minutes is encouraged but optional."
* "A source-code or project-file link where possible. Credit third-party libraries, datasets, assets, AI assistance, and pre-existing work."
* "Keep required links accessible to judges throughout November 21–25. Explain limitations honestly. Submit one final entry per project before November 20 at 5:00 PM Eastern Time."

## New-work rule

Rules, "Original work and attribution": "Submit one entry per project. Clearly distinguish work completed during the event from pre-existing work." The overview asks entrants to "Clearly identify the work completed during October 16–November 20, 2026." All Ainooru code is written from 4 Nov 2026 onward in a fresh repository. The git history is the evidence.

## AI-assistance disclosure rule

Rules: "Libraries, open-source components, datasets, low-code tools, and AI assistance may be used when their terms permit it; disclose and credit them." The overview also asks entrants to "Credit third-party libraries, datasets, assets, AI assistance, and pre-existing work." The Devpost page must therefore include a "Built with and credits" section that discloses any AI assistance used during development, and states that the optional meter-photo reader calls an open model hosted on Groq.

## Entering the same project elsewhere

The rules say nothing that forbids entering the same project in another hackathon. They require "one entry per project" within this event and honest separation of pre-existing work. The owner's other entries (Padi for AccessED, Kavach for SafeSpace, Haat and Merged for NEAR) are different projects, so this is moot. Ainooru is a new idea and will not be submitted to any other event.

## Other constraints worth obeying

* No secrets, private credentials or personal data in the repo, screenshots or video. The Groq key lives only in the gitignored `.env`.
* Judging links must stay up from 21 to 25 Nov. Plan: a public source repo plus a recorded walkthrough, with a hosted demo if a free host without a card is available.
* Resources page: "No paid software or specific platform is required. Check free-tier limits and third-party terms before choosing a service."
* Conduct: no plagiarism or interference with other projects.

## What wins at this organiser and similar events

* Hack4TodayOrg has no ended events on Devpost. Its galleries for LaunchPad, AccessED, SafeSpace and Green Shift are all unpublished ("The hackathon managers haven't published this gallery yet").
* The closest comparable is Hack to the Future (HackathonForEveryone, Oct 2026, ended), which uses the same beginner-friendly format. Most of its 24 entries are focused, single-problem tools tied to a real product or place: "RailDrop: live order window for IRCTC eCatering", "Google Classroom: My Tracker + Hidden Deadlines", "FairQueue for Spotify Jam", and "Change Map", which opens with "Research on 23,241 pull requests found...". Generic "AI productivity companion" entries are common and blend together.
* Neighborhood Hacks 2026 (646 participants) shows the same pattern. The specific local tools ("Is it an e-bike?", "Yukikaki Link", "PillPulse", "FlatDesk") stand out against generic study planners.
* Student Innovators Without Borders (ended, winners marked) rewarded concrete, evidence-backed builds with an identifiable user (SafeWander, Project Kisan, Bionic Reader).
* Takeaway: these judges reward one sharp, real, local problem, evidence that the need exists, a working core loop, honest limitations and a clear story. Breadth does not score.
